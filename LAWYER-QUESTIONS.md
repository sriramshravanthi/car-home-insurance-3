# Questions for your lawyer

Bring the whole `site/` folder and this list. These are questions, not answers: nothing here is legal advice.

## Licensing and what the site is
1. Does an educational tool that estimates prices, checks coverage, suggests coverage levels and tells users what to ask an agent count as "insurance advice", "consulting" or "solicitation" in the states where you want to reach users? Is a producer license or a consultant license needed?
2. Do the coverage suggestions (for example "your liability limits look low", "consider flood insurance") need different wording or a stronger disclaimer?
3. If you later add referral links, ads, lead capture or "get a quote", what licensing and disclosure rules apply?
4. Are the words "estimate", "typical range" and the "percentile" line acceptable, or do any state rules on rate comparisons or advertising apply?

## Terms, disclaimers and liability
5. Will the draft disclaimers and limitation of liability be enforceable against consumers? What changes for states with strong consumer-protection rules?
6. Should the terms use a clickwrap (users must click to agree) or is a browsewrap link enough for an educational site?
7. Should the site carry a visible "last updated" notice on each result, and does the "learning tool, not a quote" wording need to appear inside the tool results as well?
8. Do you need errors-and-omissions or general liability insurance for the operator?
9. What is the right governing law, venue and dispute clause?

## Data and content
10. Each source's figures are shown with attribution and the workbook and downloads include compiled figures. For each source (NerdWallet, Insurance.com, InsuranceQuotes, Bankrate, MoneyGeek, Insure.com, Insurify, LexisNexis, J.D. Power, the Insurance Information Institute summary), do its terms allow this use? Are facts and figures free to use, and when do we need permission?
11. The Oklahoma Insurance Department's public rate comparison tables are republished in the downloads with insurer names and prices. Is that use of state government data acceptable? Any concern about naming insurers?
12. Are the statements about insurers, regulators and rules (for example the Florida roof-age rule as summarized by roofing contractors, or the FAIR Plan description) accurately worded, or should they be softened or removed until checked against statutes?
13. Trademark: is "Coverage Check" (or your chosen name) clear to use? Do we need permission to use or link to "NAIC", "FEMA", or the names of data sources?

## Privacy and security
14. The tool does not send entered data anywhere and uses no cookies, storage or third-party scripts. Does the privacy policy meet the laws that apply to you (for example California and other state privacy laws, and rules for children)? Do server logs at the host count as personal data?
15. Do you need a cookie or tracking notice if none is used? What changes if analytics is added?
16. Are there breach-notification duties for a site that holds no user data except email and host logs?

## Accessibility
17. What accessibility standard and legal exposure applies (for example ADA claims and state rules)? Is the draft statement wording safe? What should it promise?

## Fairness and AI
18. The site describes the NAIC model bulletin, New York's Circular Letter No. 7 and Colorado's SB21-169 as background. These apply to insurers. Do any of them, or state consumer-protection rules on automated tools, apply to this site? Should the "Trust and fairness" tools be described differently?

## Operations
19. What records should you keep (versions, complaints, error reports) and for how long?
20. What is the process for correcting a wrong number that someone acted on?
