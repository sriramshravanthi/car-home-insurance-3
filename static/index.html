The reality check

Why these numbers deserve a look

Every line below links to the underlying source, not just our word for it.

01

Insurers disagree — a lot. In the Oklahoma Insurance Department's official samples, the highest insurer charged about 4.7 to 4.9 times the lowest for the same driver or home. Source

02

Prices vary enormously by state. Published car insurance medians run from $1,148 a year in Wyoming to $4,296 in Louisiana. Source

03

Homes are often underinsured. One survey of 3,000 policyholders found three in four were underinsured; published rebuild costs run from $162 to $350 a square foot. Source

04

Storm deductibles can be large. A 2% hurricane deductible on a $560,000 house is $11,200 — while the average wind or hail claim is about $13,500. Source

05

When prices rise, people cut protection. J.D. Power reports financially stretched drivers, especially younger ones, more often cut coverage or lapse a policy than shop around. Source

Questions

Before you start

Is this a real insurance quote?

No. It's an estimate from published benchmarks and a model tested against one state's official insurer prices. Only a real quote from an insurer shows what you'd actually pay.

Do you sell my information or show ads?

No. This version has no ads, no referral links, no lead forms and no accounts. What you type stays in your browser.

Are you insurance agents or a licensed brokerage?

No. Coverage Check is an educational tool. It does not sell, broker, or bind insurance, and does not replace a licensed agent or your state insurance department.

Where do the numbers actually come from?

Public sources like NerdWallet, Insurance.com, the Oklahoma Insurance Department, FEMA, and J.D. Power — every one listed with a link on the sources page. Where there's no published figure, we say so.

Ready to see where you stand?

Two minutes, no sign-up, and you'll know exactly which numbers to double-check with a real agent.
