How it works and how it was tested

Every number comes from a published source or is labelled as an assumption. This page explains the method, the testing, and the limits.

A learning tool, not a quote. Prices are estimates from published benchmarks and a model tested against one state's official insurer prices. Only a real quote shows what you would pay. Confirm anything you act on with a licensed agent and your state insurance department.

1. What it is, and is not

It helps a household check a car or home policy against published benchmarks and coverage rules. It is not a quote engine, it cannot see your address or your insurer's rules, and it makes no decision about you.

2. Where prices come from

  • Car: each state's published median full-coverage premium (NerdWallet, September 2026, from public filings) for a 35-year-old with a clean record and good credit, adjusted for your answers.
  • Home: each state's published average at four coverage levels (Insurance.com, September 15, 2026), interpolated to your house limit, then adjusted.
  • Where a factor has a published figure the tool uses it and says so. Where it does not, the factor is our assumption and is labelled in the result's "decision receipt".

4. The range and the percentile

The "typical range" is the middle half of expected insurer prices, about x0.74 to x1.35 for cars and x0.73 to x1.37 for homes. It comes from how much insurers differ for the same customer in Oklahoma's official samples, plus an allowance for the tool's own error. The percentile says where your price sits within that spread. Both are rough, and both borrow Oklahoma's spread for every state.

6. Coverage checks

These use rules and arithmetic on your answers, with published average claim sizes (fire $83,991; liability $31,690; water damage $13,954; wind and hail $13,511; theft $5,024). Rows labelled "illustrative" in the home stress test use round loss amounts we chose.

7. Fairness and governance

  • The tool includes a model card, a controls register, a rules audit, and screens for fairness and drift that you can run on your own data (in the tool's "Trust and fairness" section).
  • Two governance controls are not built: a named owner and an incident process. See the model card in the tool.
  • Regulators expect insurers to document, test and monitor models, including for unfair discrimination (for example the NAIC model bulletin, New York's Circular Letter No. 7 and Colorado's SB21-169). This tool does not set anyone's price, but it follows the same practice. Sources

8. What it cannot do

  • It cannot see your address, roof, insurer's rules, credit report or claims history.
  • State-level only: within a state, the same profile's price moved by up to about 16% (car) and 22% (home) between cities in Oklahoma's samples.
  • Home prices are one source's view; a second source is 25% to 60% lower in the ten states compared.
  • It is not validated on real quotes outside Oklahoma's official samples.
  • It does not know your policy wording. Your policy and your insurer control what is paid.
