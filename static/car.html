What the car check covers

Price

An estimate, range and percentile for all 50 states and D.C., adjusted for age, driving record, credit (where your state allows it), coverage, deductible, mileage and use.

Liability check

Your state's legal minimum, your limits, and a suggested level based on what you could lose. Legal is not the same as safe.

Uninsured & underinsured drivers

Whether your state requires this cover, and what it pays if the other driver has little or no insurance.

No-fault states

A plain-language explanation of personal injury protection and lawsuit limits, for the 12 states that use them.

After a DUI

What an SR-22 or FR-44 is, and how long it is usually needed.

Discounts & telematics

The discounts you may qualify for, with reported ranges, and the risks of usage-based programs.

Renewal check

Whether a renewal offer looks high, and how long a higher deductible takes to pay off.

Have these ready

✓Your policy price (monthly or yearly), coverage type and deductible.
✓Your liability limits, on your policy's "declarations page" (three numbers such as 50/100/50).
✓Your car's rough value, whether you still owe money on it, and your annual mileage.
✓Your driving record for the past three to five years.

Good to know

1

Car insurance rates depend on your state. Published medians run from $1,148 a year in Wyoming to $4,296 in Louisiana for a 35-year-old with a clean record and good credit.

2

Credit is ignored in California, Hawaii, Massachusetts and Michigan, where it cannot be used to set car prices.

3

The tool does not use your gender, marital status, occupation, income, education or ZIP code.

4

Your city can move the price by about 15% either way; the tool uses a state-level estimate.

A learning tool, not a quote. Prices are estimates from published benchmarks and a model tested against one state's official insurer prices. Only a real quote shows what you would pay. Confirm anything you act on with a licensed agent and your state insurance department.
